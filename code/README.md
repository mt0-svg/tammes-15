# Checks of the paper

Every computational claim of the paper is listed below with the script that checks it and its complete recorded output. `code/rerun.sh NAME ...` reruns a check by name and compares its output with the recorded one (timings and PARI's stack warnings removed), or, for the checks without a recorded output, relies on the exit status of a script that compares the results itself. `code/rerun.sh --list` prints the names; `SAVE=dir code/rerun.sh ...` also keeps the outputs.

Layout: `impl1/` is the first implementation (the plantri output filter, the guide of plantri 5.8 (`plantri-guide.txt`, unchanged) and the enumeration scripts in `impl1/enum/`, the Rust programs of the search, the certificates and the replay in `impl1/rust/`, the recorded outputs in `impl1/out/`); `impl2/` is the second implementation, described in `impl2/README.md`; `gp/` and `sage/` hold the PARI/GP and SageMath checks with their outputs (`.out` next to each script); `fg/` is the second enumerator of Section 9.3, and `d2d3/` holds its runs against plantri at n = 12 to 15; `plantri-ms-check/` checks plantri against the formula of Mullin and Schellenberg; `lean/` holds the checks of the Lean package, and `lean/difftest/` the differential test of Section 9.3.

Times are wall clock on an AMD Ryzen 9 5900X (24 threads), with `J` parallel jobs where stated, while other work was running. They were measured on the recorded runs and are indications only: no recorded output holds them, and `code/rerun.sh` prints the time of each check it reruns. The continuous integration runs every check marked CI (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`, 4-core runners). The checks of the group assets read the unpacked release assets (`ASSETS=dir code/rerun.sh NAME`, see `ASSETS.md`); the job `assets` of `ci.yml` runs them on the tarballs of the release, or of its draft before the release is published.

The recorded PARI/GP outputs were made with PARI/GP 2.17.4; the gp job of CI installs PARI/GP 2.15.4 (Ubuntu 24.04) and compares its outputs with the recorded ones.

## Continuous integration

The workflow `ci.yml` runs on GitHub's runners at each push that touches the Lean package, the code or the data, and when started by hand. Its first job reads the files of the pushed commits: the Lean jobs run when they touch a Lean source, the Lake files, `config.json` or `code/lean/`, the computation jobs when they touch `code/` outside `code/lean/`, `data/` or `ASSETS.md`; a change of `ci.yml`, a new branch, a pull request and a run by hand run every job. The Lean jobs run `code/lean/check.sh` (the build of the package, compiling only what changed since the last build, restored from the cache or from the latest release; every target up to date; a scan of the sources for the constructs that can bypass the kernel; `#print axioms` of the theorems of `code/lean/axioms.lean`, `Tammes15.conjecture_of_enum_killed` among them; the types of the interfaces; an audit of every constant of the package), a scan for `sorry`, `admit` and `native_decide`, and Comparator on the theorems of `config.json`, in nanoda alone (`lean_kernel` false in `config.json`, a patch of this repository on Comparator), with its seven controls; a run of Comparator whose exporter crashes during the export, before any kernel runs, is repeated once, and the repetition decides. The computation jobs rerun the computations marked CI below and compare each output with the recorded one: the enumeration and the first level of both programs on every part, the replay of a sample of certificates, the second program on a sample and on the graphs left by its bulk passes, the SageMath and PARI/GP checks with those of `code/hyps/`, and the replay of every certificate of the second level in three parallel jobs, followed by the totals and the coverage join on these replays (the longest replay jobs take about 55 min each by their recorded times); when a release holds the data tarballs of `ASSETS.md`, it also runs the checks that read them (a run started by hand fails without them). On a tag, `release.yml` takes the `ci.yml` run started by hand on the tagged commit, checks through the GitHub API that every job of that run except the badges passed, and attaches the PDF, the outputs of its checks and the build that Comparator checked to the release; it compiles nothing but the PDF, and takes the data tarballs of the previous release when `ASSETS.md` has not changed. A run started by hand on `main` writes the three badges of `README.md` from its jobs: the hypotheses and axioms of the theorems of `config.json` (`code/lean/facts.lean`), the kernels of Comparator, and the computation jobs that passed; each badge links to that run.
