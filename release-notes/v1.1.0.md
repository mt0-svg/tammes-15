A new version of the paper and the Lean package of v1.0.0: the slightly weaker form of the bound of Fejes Tóth that the reduction uses is now proved in Lean, so the main theorem takes the four finite computations D1 to D4 as its only hypotheses.

The largest possible minimal angular distance between fifteen points on the unit sphere is arccos u ≈ 53.6578501°, where u is the root in (1/2, 7/10) of `13u^5 - u^4 + 6u^3 + 2u^2 - 3u - 1`; this value is attained by two configurations described by Buddenhagen and Kottwitz, and their contact graphs are not isomorphic.

This is AI-generated research: the results, proofs and code were found and written by AI. Credit goes to all the humans whose work it builds on.

**Proved in Lean 4:** `Tammes15.reduction`, with Mathlib and only the standard axioms, proves `Tammes15.Conjecture` from four hypotheses: `KappaHyp` (D1, the rigidity bound), `EnumComplete` (D2, the completeness of the list of plane graphs), `Killed` (D3, the refutation of every graph) and `AttainedHyp` (D4, the attainment). The bound d₁₅ ≤ 56.6716°, slightly weaker than that of Fejes Tóth, is the theorem `Tammes15.fejesToth_bound : Tammes15.FejesTothBound`, with no hypothesis. Comparator checks both theorems against `Tammes15/Challenge.lean` with the Lean kernel and nanoda. **By computation:** D1 to D4, by programs that are not verified in Lean, as in v1.0.0. `code/rerun.sh NAME` reruns a check and compares its output with the recorded one; `code/README.md` lists every check with its script and recorded output.

Changes since v1.0.0:

- Lean: the modules `Tammes15/FejesToth/` prove `FejesTothBound`, whose definition is unchanged: a configuration at a distance above 56.6716° is extended to a saturated set at the level c₀ = 0.5494377; every fan triangle of every facet of its convex hull, the hull of the proof of Corollary twoconn, has angle sum at least π + 2ϑ(c₀), that of the equilateral triangle of side arccos c₀ (a triangle lemma proved by polynomial identities); Euler's relation then gives (2|Y| - 4) 2ϑ(c₀) ≤ 4π for the saturated set Y, which contains the fifteen points, against ϑ(c₀) > π/13. The proof uses the spherical triangles and polygons of the vendored eight-point code of Kryvonos, Liehr and Taylor. `Tammes15.reduction` no longer takes `(h0 : Tammes15.FejesTothBound)`.
- Paper: new Section 8.4, the formal proof of the bound (Lemmas 8.1 and 8.2, Proposition 8.3, the numerical facts N1 and N2); Sections 8.4 and 8.5 of v1.0.0 are now 8.5 and 8.6. The abstract, the introduction, Sections 3, 6, 7 and 8, the list of remarks and the appendix table say that the bound is proved in Lean. The written proof of Section 3 still cites Fejes Tóth (1943); the Lean proof follows another route, after Section 5.2 of the book *Lagerungen* of L. Fejes Tóth, G. Fejes Tóth and W. Kuperberg (2023), now cited. Every numbered statement now has the hypotheses of its Lean declaration, and each Lean line names what the declaration assumes: Lemma 3.12 assumes edges of one length, Lemmas 5.2 and 8.1 are stated as in Lean, and D1 is named as the weaker form of Lemma 4.2. Lemma 3.13, Proposition 3.16 and Section 7 have the proofs that the Lean proof follows: a linear bound along the arc; the angles subtended at a rattler, which was Remark 3.21 of v1.0.0, so that Lemma 3.15 keeps only its part on two discs and Remark 3.22 is now 3.21; and the contradiction from Pair and Local on the glued configuration.
- Comparator: the challenge states `Tammes15.reduction` and `Tammes15.fejesToth_bound`. `code/lean/check.sh` allows the two `sorry` of the challenge statements, and `code/lean/axioms.lean` prints the axioms of the bound and of 16 theorems of its proof.
- CI: a first job of `ci.yml` reads the files of the pushed commits, and a push runs only the Lean jobs or the computation jobs that they touch; a change of `ci.yml`, a new branch, a pull request and a run by hand run every job. `release.yml` takes the green run started by hand on the tagged commit.
