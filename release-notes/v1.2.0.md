A new version of the paper and the Lean package. The rigidity bound D1 and the attainment D4 are now proved in Lean, so the main theorem and the non-uniqueness of the optimum (Corollary 1.2) take only the two finite statements D2 (the completeness of the list of plane graphs) and D3 (the refutation of every graph) as hypotheses. The step from the outputs of the first program to D3 is proved in Lean as well: the contractors of the program are written over an arbitrary arithmetic and proved sound for every sound arithmetic, so that the main theorem follows from D2, the trees that the replays of the program accept, and interval procedures computed by the program (Theorem B.9).

The largest possible minimal angular distance between fifteen points on the unit sphere is arccos u ≈ 53.65785°, where u is the root in (1/2, 7/10) of `13u^5 - u^4 + 6u^3 + 2u^2 - 3u - 1`; this value is attained by two configurations described by Buddenhagen and Kottwitz, and their contact graphs are not isomorphic.

This is AI-generated research: the results, proofs and code were found and written by AI. Credit goes to all the humans whose work it builds on.

**Proved in Lean 4:** `Tammes15.conjecture_of_enum_progTreesDom (L) (N : PaperSteps.Procs) (h2 : EnumComplete L) (h3 : Contractors.ProgTreesDom L N) (hN : Contractors.Impl N) : Conjecture` (Theorem B.9), with Mathlib and only the standard axioms: the value of d₁₅ from D2, from trees accepted by the procedures `N` from boxes of the domain that contain the root boxes, and from `N` computed by the program in the sense of Definition B.1. `Tammes15.nonunique_of_enum_progTreesDom`, from the same hypotheses: two configurations attain the maximum and their contact graphs are not isomorphic. `Tammes15.conjecture_of_enum_killed (L) (h2 : EnumComplete L) (h3 : Killed L {Attained.frameC1, Attained.frameC3}) : Conjecture` (Theorem B.8) and `Tammes15.nonunique_of_enum_killed` prove the same from D2 and D3. The proofs contain the structure theorem, the inequality of the local optimality theorem, D1 for the two optima (`Tammes15.Kappa.kappaHyp`), D4 for them (`Tammes15.Attained.attained`), the numerical margins and the bound d₁₅ ≤ 56.6716° (`Tammes15.fejesToth_bound`). Comparator checks these four theorems and eleven more, among them `Tammes15.reduction` (from D1 to D4), `Tammes15.fejesToth_bound` and `Tammes15.Contractors.killed_of_progTreesDom` (D3 from the hypotheses of Theorem B.9), against `Tammes15/Challenge.lean` in the CI run started by hand on the tagged commit, in nanoda alone. **By computation:** D2 by plantri, and the trees by the first level of the first program and the replay of its certificates, by programs that are not verified in Lean. **Outside Lean:** the four statements of Section 9.7 of the paper: D2 read from the description of plantri, the soundness of the arithmetic of the program, the agreement of its narrowings with the primitive steps of the formalization, and the acceptance of the trees by its first level and its replay. `code/rerun.sh NAME` reruns a check and compares its output with the recorded one; `code/README.md` lists every check with its script and recorded output.

Changes since v1.1.0:

- Lean: `Tammes15/Kappa/` proves D1 for the two optima by an exact certificate: a quadratic form, nonnegative on the space orthogonal to the rotations up to an error term, whose integer matrix is checked in the kernel, for the frame rounded to multiples of 2⁻¹⁰⁰. `Tammes15/Attained/` proves D4 for them: the unit norms and the thirty contacts of each are polynomial identities in the field of degree 20, proved with cofactors of a Gröbner basis, and every other inner product is at most u, by interval bounds with dyadic rationals on a box that contains the roots. `Tammes15/Params/` proves the inequalities of the SageMath check of the constants: the enclosures of the constants of the runs, the upper end of the range of arccos u, the margin above the value of Fejes Tóth and the margin of the convex hull proof of Proposition 3.17 of v1.1.0 on each of its 40 intervals (the other three margins of Section 4.3 were proved in v1.1.0). `Tammes15/Nonunique/` proves from D2 and D3 that two maximal configurations, C1 and C3 in the proof, have contact graphs that are not isomorphic (Corollary 1.2 with the configurations unnamed in the statement). The non-isomorphism of the contact graphs of C1 and C3, an edge for each pair at inner product exactly u, is `Tammes15.Nonunique.frames_not_iso`, with no hypothesis; it needs the strict separation of every other pair. `Tammes15/PaperSteps/` proves the steps of Appendix B.3 from the verdicts of the program to D3: Lemma B.7, from the verdicts of the program in its own conventions (`ProgKilled`) to D3, through a relabelling of the hexagons, a reflection between the two gluings and the containment of the frame points in the boxes of the data of Local; Proposition 6.4 for sound interval procedures and accepted trees (`progKilled_of_progTrees`); the main theorem from D2 and such trees (`conjecture_of_enum_progTrees`). It also proves Theorem C with its equality case (`local_optimality_frame`), item (3) of Proposition 3.1 on sets of points (`optima_four`) and the last sentence of Corollary 1.2 (`frames_not_isometric`, `frames_not_distance_preserving`). `Tammes15/Contractors/` defines the arithmetic, the primitive steps and the runs of Definition B.1, with the two guards of Appendix B.1, and proves Proposition B.2 (procedures computed by the program, restricted to the domain, are sound) and Theorem B.9; `Tammes15/Contractors/DiffTest/` holds a copy of the primitive steps over the rationals with an arithmetic given by a table, proved to compute what the primitive steps compute for each of 31 functions. `Tammes15/D2Draw/` and `Tammes15/D2Regions/` prove Lemma 4.9 (the regions of the drawing of the contact graph are the insides of its face walks) and that the rotation system of a structured configuration is in the class of D2, which is now stated on drawings (Definition 7.1). The certificate data, the witnesses of `Tammes15/Params/`, the strict separations and the data of Local are written by the scripts of `code/lean-data/`; `code/rerun.sh lean_data` writes them again and compares them with the Lean files, byte for byte for the generated modules.
- Programs: the first program carries two guards (`code/impl1/deep-guards.patch`), on the triangle angle of (T2) and on the side of (T9), which the soundness of these contractors over an arbitrary sound arithmetic requires; with them it replayed every tree with the same result as the program as first published (`code/impl1/out/guarded/`).
- Paper: D1 and D4 are proved (Lemma 5.1 by its certificate, Proposition 3.1 by the identities and interval bounds of the formal proof). The paper is reorganized after the model of Musin and Tarasov for N = 14: Section 2 proves Theorem 1.1 from three statements, Proposition A (a maximal configuration gives a realisation of a case of a graph of the class), Proposition B (such a realisation is close to an optimum) and Theorem C (local optimality), and the later sections prove them in turn. Section 4 states the structure facts with their classical sources and proves Theorem 4.1 and Proposition A from them; their proofs are in Appendix A. Section 7 states D2 and D3 in mathematics (Definitions 7.1 to 7.3), reports the computation and proves Proposition B from Proposition 7.7, the two finite statements for the list of plantri. Appendix B holds what connects the programs to the paper: the arithmetic and the formal model of the program with its two guards (Definition B.1, Proposition B.2), D2 read from the description of plantri (Proposition B.3), D3 from the verdicts of the program (Definition B.6, Lemma B.7), and the two formal statements, from D2 and D3 (Theorem B.8) and from D2, the trees that the replays accept and procedures computed by the program (Theorem B.9). The bound of Fejes Tóth is Appendix C. Figure 1 draws the contact graphs of C1 and C3, Figure 2 the darts and corners at a vertex and Figure 3 the polygon of Lemma A.11; the convexity of that polygon is written out in its proof. Letters with two meanings are renamed (the frame rotations are ζ and χ, the dihedral group Δ). The numerical rechecks of statements that Lean proves, and the tests on the realised subgraphs of the two optima, are gone from the paper, and their scripts and outputs from the repository. The body has no code, no listing and no path: each proof that rests on the formalization or a computation names one declaration or script in its last sentence, and the map from every numbered statement to its declarations and scripts is the table of `STATEMENTS.md`, generated from macros of the TeX source by `code/paper/statement_map.pl`. A closing Section 9, "Computations and formal proof", gives the software and the machine, the checks with their running times, the second implementations, the formal proof, the vendored eight-point code, where the formal route differs, what the result relies on, and the data. The written proofs of Corollary A.6 and Proposition 4.6 are those of the formal proof: the convex hull of the vertices of the contact graph with its facets, counted by a genus inequality for permutations (Lemmas A.4 and A.5), and an inscribed polygon in place of the convex hull of two discs; the proofs by the formula of Gauss and Bonnet and by the convex hull of two discs, and the build option `\UsePolygonForm`, are gone. The guide of plantri 5.8 is in the repository (`code/impl1/enum/plantri-guide.txt`), and the bound of Fejes Tóth is quoted for comparison only. The appendix table is gone, replaced by `STATEMENTS.md`.
